# Owner onboarding

## First move

Read AGENTS.md, mi.md, Home.md and AIOS/onboarding.md. Check private working-copy location before saving business details. Resume from the checkpoint; never restart a completed interview. Explain: “We will take this one task at a time. You can stop and come back whenever you like.”

Read the sibling grill-me/SKILL.md and use it to conduct the interview.

## Interview with persistence, not pressure

Ask ONE question at a time. Begin with yesterday's actual work, from first task to finish. Follow each concrete example until the workflow is understood. Do not dump a questionnaire, praise every answer or make the owner learn technical vocabulary. Let them skip and revisit a branch.

For each workflow discover, as relevant: trigger; source/input; actual steps; people/roles; tools; output; deadline; frequency; manual effort; approval; exceptions; mistakes; authoritative record; desired result. Ask about a real example, with sensitive details removed where possible.

Branch from answers into clients, requirements, candidate handling, team oversight, meetings, follow-ups, documents and business administration. Do not assume every branch applies. Explore Outlook/Microsoft and WhatsApp usage through examples; defer technical configuration until a useful workflow needs it. Ask about device and account access rather than guessing.

After a few answers, summarise in plain language: “Here is how I understand this task...” Ask what is wrong or missing. Persist confirmed facts, unresolved questions, the current branch and exactly the next question in AIOS/onboarding.md. Avoid asking things files already answer. Mark unconfirmed facts explicitly.

## Deliver one early win

As soon as a small task is clear, help with it: a reply draft, a missing-information checklist, call follow-up or document outline. Show the result and ask what they would change. Record their corrections as preferences. Continue discovery afterwards; do not require completing the interview before useful help.

## Turn understanding into a small setup

Store preferences in mi.md, business context and procedures in Atlas, commitments in Efforts, priorities in Home. Use templates. Never move or rewrite original Raw sources. Keep operational transaction records in their existing systems.

For a repeated task, explain the proposed workflow with their example, then ask whether it matches their work. Create a relevant skill only after shared understanding. Use `.agents/skills/<lowercase-hyphenated-name>/SKILL.md` with name/description/generated-by frontmatter. Specify trigger, inputs, steps, output, exceptions, decision boundaries and what it does without connectors. Update AIOS/skills-map.md and mirror identical files into `.claude/skills/` for ZIP/Windows compatibility. Follow existing skill setup conventions; do not install global dependencies as a side effect.

Recommend a new tool only when existing tools cannot solve a confirmed need. Research current official docs. Explain one recommendation, its setup, cost if verified, limitations and a simpler fallback. Never impose a technology menu on the owner. Draft before external actions.

## Completion

The interview ends when important workflow branches have been covered or explicitly deferred, the owner confirms your summary, and one useful workflow has been tried. Record deferred branches. Ask what would make the next fortnight successful; measure actual effort saved without promising a multiplier. Save via vault-save and leave one clear next action.
